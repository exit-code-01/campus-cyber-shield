Can you spot the scam?

You will see 12 real-looking messages (SMS, emails and WhatsApp chats) that students in India actually receive. Some are genuine, some are phishing. Decide which is which. After every answer you will see the red flags that give a scam away.

How to play

  3. Choose ✅ Genuine or 🚩 Phishing.

📖 Red Flag Guide: the SLAM method

Before you click or reply, SLAM the message: check these 4 things.

S Sender

  • Check the real address or number, not just the display name.
  • Look-alike spellings: lnstagram, amaz0n.
  • Official bodies don't use Gmail/Outlook addresses.
  • Banks send SMS from sender IDs like JM-SBIBNK-T, never from a 10-digit mobile number.

L Links

  • Hover or long-press to see where a link really goes.
  • Read the website name from the right: in indiapost.gov.in-track.top the real owner is in-track.top.
  • Indian government sites end in .gov.in / .nic.in; banks now use .bank.in.
  • Short links (bit.ly etc.) hide the destination.

A Attachments

  • Never open .apk, .exe, .html or .zip files from unknown senders.
  • A "wedding card" or "question paper" ending in .apk is an app, not a document.
  • Install apps only from the Play Store / App Store.

M Message

  • Urgency or threats: "blocked today", "within 24 hours".
  • Too good to be true: prizes, easy money, scholarships you never applied for.
  • Asks for OTP, UPI PIN, password or a "fee".
  • Generic greeting ("Dear Customer"), spelling mistakes.

🌟 Golden rules

  • Never share an OTP or UPI PIN with anyone, not even friends or "bank officers".
  • You never need a UPI PIN to receive money.
  • When in doubt, don't click. Open the official app or type the website yourself.
  • Ask: "What does this message want me to DO?"

📞 Where to report (India)

  • Lost money? Call 1930 immediately or report at cybercrime.gov.in
  • Suspicious call / SMS / WhatsApp (no money lost)? Report on Chakshu at sancharsaathi.gov.in
